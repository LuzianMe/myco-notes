# Changelog

## 0.46.2
- **A benchmark that runs by itself: `graph.html?bench=1`** (or **Run benchmark** in the `?perf=1` panel). With nobody touching the mouse it runs four situations, each for six seconds after a short warm-up: left alone, turning by itself, turned by hand (a steady drag) and with the most-mentioned note selected; once drawn by the graphics card (where WebGL 2 exists) and once by the processor. Nothing steps down during it. At the end a table shows the frame rate, the time 95% of frames come in under, the share of slow frames, the page's own work and the pace the page chose, and the result is saved as `myco-graph-bench-<date>.json` (the browser may ask for the download; a **Download the results** button is there too, and **Copy**). The file holds the numbers, the graphics card, the window and canvas size and the 2D canvas test; no note name, path or text. `?quick=1` shortens each situation to 1.5 seconds.
- **A slow machine lowers the card's resolution**: after the pixel density step (screens with 2 device pixels per pixel), when full-pace frames stay more than 40 ms apart for three seconds, the card's canvas drops to 75% and later to 60% of the page's size, scaled up by the browser (a little softer, far fewer pixels to fill). It never goes back. `?scale=0.5` starts the card at a given size (40% to 100%).
- **`?glow=0`** leaves out the soft glow of the threads and the notes on the card, to see what the glow costs on a machine.
- **`?perf=1` says why it stays at full pace** (`frame pace now: full (camera)`) and its one-line numbers carry `paceWhy`, `glScale` and `glow`.
- **Tests**: `tests/browser.py` runs the benchmark (`?bench=1&quick=1`: every situation in both ways of drawing, a file-ready result without note names, the table and buttons, Close), `?scale=0.5&glow=0` and the governor's resolution steps under a throttled processor.

## 0.46.1
- **Calm pacing: the view draws only as fast as it needs to.** A laptop that drew the graph at 60 frames a second all the time, even when nothing moved, got hot, its fan started and its clock dropped (one laptop went from 60 to 19 frames a second within minutes, with 4 ms of the page's own work per frame). Now a frame is drawn at once while something moves or the reader is at work (dragging, zooming, hovering, a turn or its coast, a replay, a settling layout, a glide of the camera, and for a second and a half after any input); at about 30 a second while only spikes, flashes, particles, the lit threads of a selected note or the screensaver run; and at about 15 a second when all that moves is the slow life of the view (names fading in and out, the pulse of recent notes). Any input wakes it at once. `?perf=1` shows the pace and why (`frame pace now: calm (names, pulse)`), and its frame numbers are those of full-pace frames only.
- **A lighter glow in dense graphs**: the threads' glow (the most expensive part per pixel and the least visible in a crowd) gets thinner and fainter as threads times pixels grow, from about 400 (threads x megapixels) down to 35% at 1600; a 157-note graph with 1056 threads on a wide window is in that range, a small one is unchanged.
- **A cheaper still frame, not an aggressive one**: the rectangles of the panels (which keep names clear of them) are measured at most four times a second while the view is paced, and the data of the threads is sent to the card again only when something that shapes it changed (the view, a note's place or size, the selection, the search, the theme). The slow-machine steps of the 2D drawing count only full-pace frames.
- **Tests**: `tests/browser.py` checks that a view left alone draws at the calm or live pace and that a moving pointer brings back full pace.

## 0.46.0
- **The graphics card draws the graph by default** where the browser has WebGL 2 (`?gl=0` switches it off and brings back the 2D drawing; Matrix, Amber terminal, embeds and browsers without WebGL 2 use the 2D drawing as before). Measured on an Intel UHD 630 laptop with a 2D canvas that is drawn on the processor (the `?perf=1` canvas test took 37 ms): 60 notes, 40 frames a second with 10% of frames over 33 ms and a worst frame of 117 ms in 2D, against 53 frames a second, no slow frames and a worst frame of 33 ms with the card.
- **Less for the browser to compose**: with the card drawing the threads, the empty layer of the 2D threads is hidden, and the 2D canvas is cleared only when something was drawn on it in the last frame (its names, spikes, particles or ripples).
- **Tests**: the pixel tests of the 2D drawing run with `?gl=0`; the default is checked with `?perf=1` (the card draws, or the fallback says so), a hidden iframe with the card, a digital theme and back, Paper, and a selected note.

## 0.45.1
- **`?gl=1` draws the notes on the graphics card too**, in the themes whose notes are plain discs (Mycelium, Deep sea, Light, Dark, High contrast): the glow, the disc with its shine, the hub's rings, the flash when a spike arrives and the dashed ring of a note past its review date are one quad per note, drawn by a shader in the same pass as the threads, back to front as before. The selected note's threads (the lit ones) are drawn by the card as well. What stays on the 2D canvas is small: the names, the spikes, the hub particles of the themes that have them, the mark of a missing reference and the replay's ripples, so the 2D canvas is nearly empty and costs little even where the browser draws 2D canvases on the processor. Themes with drawn notes (Fruit, Paper) keep the notes on the 2D canvas with the threads on the card; Matrix and Amber terminal stay entirely on the 2D drawing. Without WebGL 2 nothing changes.
- **`?perf=1` shows a 2D canvas test**: how long a fixed job of 150 translucent sprites takes on a 900 x 900 canvas here. A few milliseconds mean a hardware-accelerated 2D canvas, tens mean the browser draws it on the processor (which is why a still view can run at 40 frames a second with 2 ms of its own work). It runs 2.5 seconds after the start, so it does not delay the first frames. The overlay also says whether the notes are on the card (`GPU threads and notes`) or not.
- **Tests**: `tests/browser.py` checks the notes on the card, Paper (threads on the card, notes on 2D), a selected note with `?gl=1` and the canvas test line.

## 0.45.0
- **The network no longer turns by itself by default.** The idle turn was the worst-looking state of the view: while it ran, the camera re-fitted to the turning picture in every frame and the threads were redrawn only every third frame, so they lagged behind the notes. Now the view stays still until you drag it (a drag still turns it, and coasts to a stop). The new **Spin** button in the tool bar (replacing the hold-still button) turns the idle turn on, and the choice is kept in the browser; `?spin=1` turns it on for one visit. The screensaver turns it while it is on, and an embedded graph still turns by itself. The standby spikes that run along a thread now run when the view has been left alone, turning or not.
- **Threads drawn by the graphics card, opt-in: `?gl=1`.** The quiet threads are a texture of paths in the layout's coordinates that a WebGL 2 shader turns, widens, fades with the depth and gives its glow and buds, in every frame; nothing is rebuilt while the view moves, so the threads move with the notes and the processor sends a few numbers per thread. The look follows the 2D drawing (tapered threads, fused two-way threads, glow, buds at the rim, depth fog); overlaps of threads of one colour are a little stronger. Notes, labels, spikes and the selected note's threads stay on the 2D canvas. It is not used for Matrix and Amber terminal, in an embed, or where WebGL 2 is missing (the 2D drawing runs as before and says so).
- **A performance overlay: `?perf=1`.** Frame rate, the time a frame's own work takes (simulation, threads, notes), pixel density, the settings that decide the cost, the graphics card, how much of the screen the thread layer paints, and a button that copies the numbers as one line, so `?gl=1` and the default can be compared on the same machine.
- **Lighter 2D drawing**: the faint side branches on long threads are gone (they were rarely visible and cost about a tenth of the frame time); the threads' glow is drawn at a quarter of the resolution (it is a blur); and the governor for slow machines has a third step, after the pixel density and the redraw rate: plain lines without glow, as an embedded graph draws them.
- **Docs and tests**: REFERENCE section 6.3 (the options of the full view), README. `tests/browser.py` checks that the view stays still by default and the Spin button, its kept choice and its turning (read from the overlay's turn angle); the overlay; and `?gl=1` (the card draws the threads and they cover about what the 2D drawing covers, a digital theme falls back to 2D and back, no error; without WebGL 2 the fallback says so).

- **Media**: `.github/assets/record_media.py` also records `wiki-layout.webp`; `--only wiki` records just that one.
- **The mycelium view draws less on a slow machine.** (1) The quiet threads are a layer of their own under the notes (a second canvas, `#t`, no pointer input) instead of being copied onto the notes' canvas in every frame; the picture is the same. (2) A governor for slow machines, step one, the pixel density: on a screen with 2 device pixels per pixel, when the frames stay more than 40 ms apart (under 25 a second) for three seconds after the first five, the density drops once to 1.5 (about 44% fewer pixels, a little softer). It never goes back, and does nothing on a screen at 1x or 1.5x, in embed mode (already capped at 1.5), while replaying, or when frames are fast. (3) If frames are still that slow, a second step, also once: while the network turns by itself its threads are redrawn every 6th frame instead of every 3rd. Measured in software-rendered Chromium at 2x: 110 notes 12.7 to 18.5 frames a second, 180 notes 9.7 to 17.7 after both steps (the density step alone: 16.3 and 13.7). The layer alone gave no gain there (the cost moved to the thread redraw); on a machine with a graphics card it saves the full-screen copy, which is not measured.
- **Tests**: `tests/native.py` checks that the four documents start with a header (a summary within 200 characters, the area). `tests/browser.py` checks the layer (under the notes, same size, no pointer input, both painted) and the governor (a throttled 2x screen drops to 1.5; no error).

## 0.44.0
- **Reviews have a folder of their own: `reviews/`** (format 1.2). A review is about the notes, not about the project, and they pile up (one or several a day), so in `plans/` they buried the real plans. New reviews go to `<notes>/reviews/review-YYYY-MM-DD.md`; the template, `AGENTS.md`, the review and ingest modes, the method (21.2) and `FORMAT.md` (3.3, 7.1, 7.5, a minor version: the old place stays valid) say so, and a new repository gets `reviews/README.md`. Nothing breaks for an existing repository: the lint, `resume`, `index --review-scope`, `ingest` and the notes map read review files in both places (`_myco.review_dirs`, `is_review_path`, `review_paths`).
- **Moving the old ones, on a yes**: `relocate.py --root PROJECT --move-reviews` is a dry run that lists the renames and the paths in the notes that name them; `--apply` does it with `git mv`, rewrites those paths (never in the history folders: logs, archive, inbox, sources, imported) and sets `FORMAT_VERSION=1.2`. It does not commit and does not edit `AGENTS.md` or `.myco-notes-map.json`; it tells the agent to propose the review line, and a map group whose prefix is `plans/`. A name that already exists in `reviews/` stops it.
- **The agent is told, once**: a refresh from the kit (`relocate.py --root PROJECT`) prints a tip when review files still sit in `plans/` (offer the move in one line; do it only on a yes), and `index --review-scope` prints a one-line note with the same words (it never turns "nothing changed" into work). `modes/setup.md` carries the rule.
- **The review scope survives the move**: the baseline of the next review is the commit that last changed the newest review's content (found with `git log --follow`), not the commit that renamed it. Only the notes whose paths were rewritten show as changed.
- **The map**: a group "Reviews of the notes" (classic and wiki layouts), drawn in the quiet colours of the other record groups in the mycelium view. The demo notes keep their review in `reviews/`.
- **Tests**: `tests/native.py` (a repository with reviews in `plans/`: scope, resume, the offer; the dry run, the same-name refusal, the move, the rewritten and the untouched paths, the format version, the scope baseline before and after, the lint, a second run, the map group), `tests/wiki.py` (the Reviews group in the tree), the behaviour scenario and the smoke test follow the new place and format. - Not asked about: folders inside the notes folder, `DATA_DIRS`, the generated map, `.github`, `.agents`, `.codex`, `.claude`, and the entry files (`AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `README.md`, `LICENSE*`, `.myco-notes*`, `.gitignore`, `.gitattributes`). Anything git already tracks, or `.gitignore` already covers, is never asked about.
- Behaviour change for existing trees: after updating the tools, a root folder that is untracked today is asked about once (answer `add` for the old behaviour, or set `NEW_FOLDERS=add`).
- Only `notes-commit` is gated; a plain `git add -A` is not.
- Tests: `tests/native.py` 84 checks, `tests/smoke.sh` with the new-folder checks added; both cover ask, each answer, `later`, the wildcard, every `NEW_FOLDERS` value and the flat layout.

## 0.6.0
- **One folder for everything.** New trees keep the notes, `tools/`, `docs/` and the generated `map/` inside one folder, `myco-notes/`; only the files agents, git and the host look for at the root (`AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `.github/`, `.gitignore`, `.gitattributes`, `.myco-notes.conf`) stay outside it. The root is left for the project's own folders. Configurable with `MYCO_DIR` (and `NOTES_DIR`, `TOOLS_DIR`, `DOCS_DIR`, `MAP_DIR` per folder). `bootstrap --layout flat` and trees without `MYCO_DIR` keep the older layout.
- **`tools/relocate`** moves an older tree into the single folder, or renames the folder: dry run by default, keeps the folder names a tree uses (for example a docs folder called `myco-docs`), rewrites the path references that name real paths, updates the conf, `.gitignore` and `core.hooksPath`, and refreshes the project's tools and docs from the kit.
- **Project data folders** (`DATA_DIRS`, `bootstrap --data-dir`): inputs, outputs and attachments that are not notes are gitignored and skipped by the lint and the map; notes may still reference files in them.
- **Lint:** `archive/` and `logs/` (`HISTORY_DIRS`) are records and are no longer re-checked for passed follow-up dates, missing references, size or orphans (secrets still are); new size check (`MAX_NOTE_KB`, default 100) and a warning when `AGENTS.md` nears the 32 KiB that Codex and some agents load (`ENTRY_WARN_KB`, default 24); references into the generated map folder are not flagged.
- **Windows fix:** the lint resolved references written relative to a note's own folder (for example `plans/backlog.md` in `state.md`) with backslashes, so on Windows every such reference was reported as missing (0.5.0 and earlier). Found by the CI run on Windows.
- The tools and launchers no longer leave `__pycache__` folders in a project.
- Tests: `tests/native.py` 60 checks, `tests/smoke.sh` 44; both cover the single-folder layout, the flat layout, `relocate`, the new lint checks and data folders.
- Verified outside the test suite: the Claude Code plugin installs on Windows, and Codex finds the skill (author's Windows machine).
